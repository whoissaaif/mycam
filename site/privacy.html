02 / Privacy

Your video
stays yours.

MyCam has no account, no server and no analytics. Video travels from your phone to your PC and stops there.

Draft — not published yet. This wording has to match the Data Safety declaration on the Google Play listing exactly, so it needs sign-off from the project owner before it goes live. Every statement below is drawn from what the code actually does; the open questions are listed at the bottom.

The short version

  • MyCam does not collect, transmit or store your video, audio or images anywhere outside your own devices.
  • There is no account and no sign-in.
  • There is no analytics, telemetry or crash reporting of any kind in the app or the companion.
  • Nothing is sold or shared, because nothing is collected.

Where the video goes

Over USB, video travels down the cable between your phone and your PC. Over Wi-Fi, it travels across your own local network, encrypted, directly between the two devices. An iPhone sends it to the page your own PC is serving, over TLS.

In every case the stream terminates on your PC, where Windows hands it to whichever app you pointed at the MyCam camera. No part of it reaches the internet, and the project operates no server that could receive it.

What is stored, and where

On your PC

  • Settings — your camera, quality, frame rate and mirror choices, in the Windows registry under your own user account.
  • Paired phones — for Wi-Fi pairing, a key per phone, stored encrypted with Windows DPAPI under your user account.
  • Paired iPhones and Android browsers — only the SHA-256 hash of each device's token, never the token itself. "Forget" removes it and ends that device's session.
  • A log file in %LOCALAPPDATA%\MyCam\, which records connection events and messages from the phone to help with troubleshooting. It stays on your machine. Nothing uploads it, and you can delete it at any time.

On your phone

  • Your preferences, and the list of PCs you have paired with.
  • A per-PC permission flag recording whether you let that PC use the camera. Scanning a pairing code is treated as consent to pair, not consent to be watched: a PC that hasn't been allowed gets a session with the camera off until you say otherwise.

Camera and microphone

  • MyCam uses the camera only while an app on your PC has the MyCam camera open, and switches it off a few seconds after that app closes it.
  • The camera also switches off by itself while Windows is locked.
  • You can pause from the phone, its notification or the PC tray. While paused, apps see a "Camera paused" picture instead of you.
  • Opening your phone's own camera app stops the MyCam stream.

Permissions, and why

  • Camera — to capture the video. That's the product.
  • Notifications (Android) — to show the streaming state and offer Pause while the service runs.
  • Local network — for the Wi-Fi and iPhone paths, to reach your PC. Only your own network, never the internet.
  • Motion sensors (iPhone page) — to tell which way up the phone is, so the picture arrives upright.
  • MyCam does not request location, contacts, storage or microphone access.

This website

The site itself is static and sets no cookies, runs no analytics and has no tracking of any kind. Being honest about what it does load from elsewhere:

  • GitHub serves the pages and the installer download, and the page asks GitHub's API for the current version. GitHub receives your IP address as part of serving any request, under their privacy statement.
  • Google Fonts serves the display typeface, and cdnjs and jsDelivr serve two animation libraries. Those requests expose your IP address to those providers.

Planned change: these files will be self-hosted so the site makes no third-party requests at all. A privacy-first product shouldn't ship a marketing page that phones elsewhere.

Children

MyCam is a general-purpose utility, is not directed at children, and collects nothing from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published here and in the repository's history, which is public — so every past version stays inspectable.

Contact

Questions, or something here that doesn't match what you observe? Open an issue on GitHub. You can also read the source: it's MIT, and every claim on this page is checkable against it.

Code signing policy

Draft. A published signing policy is a requirement for SignPath's free certificates for open-source projects. The roles and names below need confirming before this is submitted.

Current state

Windows installers for MyCam are not yet code signed. Until a certificate is in place, Windows SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC" on first run. Verify your download by comparing its SHA-256 with the hash published beside the installer on the home page and on the GitHub release.

How releases are produced

  • All source is public at github.com/whoissaaif/mycam under the MIT license.
  • Releases are built from a tagged commit by the project owner, and published as GitHub release assets with their hashes.
  • Continuous integration builds and tests both the Android app and the Windows components on every change.

Who may authorise a signed release

Only the project owner may tag a version and publish release binaries. Signing credentials are never committed to the repository and are held outside it.
