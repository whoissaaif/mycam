Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know about using MyCam on your Windows PC and phone.

Still need help? Open an issue on GitHub

MyCam uses a direct connection between your phone and Windows PC — over USB, Wi-Fi, or a browser connection for iPhone — to stream your phone's camera as a virtual webcam. No extra hardware or complex setup is required.

Nearly every failed connection is one of these four:

  • The phone never allowed the cable. Android asks once, and it is easy to miss. Unplug the phone, plug it back in and tap OK, ticking Always so it stops asking.
  • The cable only charges. Use the one that came with the phone, and if the phone asks what the connection is for, choose file transfer rather than charging.
  • The phone and PC are on different networks. Over Wi-Fi they must be on the same one, and guest or public Wi-Fi usually keeps devices apart on purpose, so MyCam can't reach across.
  • Windows calls the network Public. That blocks the phone from reaching the PC. Set the network to Private in Windows Settings, under Network & internet.

The same list is in both apps: on the PC under Devices, and on the phone under Connect. Still stuck? Write to support@mycam.dev.

No. MyCam works entirely over USB or your local Wi-Fi network — video never leaves your own devices, and no internet connection is required to stream.

MyCam only ever offers what your phone actually reports it can do — up to 4K resolution and up to 120 FPS on phones that support it. Resolution and frame rate are chosen on the PC from your phone's real capabilities, so available options vary by device.

Yes. You can switch between your phone's front and back cameras directly from the PC at any time, along with zoom, focus, and torch.

Any app that lets you choose a webcam — Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Discord, OBS, Google Meet, and the Windows Camera app included. MyCam shows up as a normal camera called "MyCam."

Yes. MyCam is completely free, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. It's open source under the MIT license — you can view the code, report issues, or contribute on GitHub.

Linux: yes, as a beta. Install it with one command, as shown on the Linux page (Ubuntu 24.04 and similar); it shows up as a camera called MyCam in Zoom, OBS, Discord and browsers, with a tray icon for the everyday controls. Android on Linux has had less testing than on Windows, so please report what you find. macOS and wired iPhone support are on the roadmap but not available yet.
